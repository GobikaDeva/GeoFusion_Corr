From Geometry as Input to Geometry as Guidance:
A Research Direction for Self-Supervised MVS

Research direction proposal, for approval · GeoFusionNet / GeoCorr project · Gobika S · 2026-10-10 · repository main @ 5572daf
Summary. The project set out to beat a self-supervised GeoMVS baseline on DTU by feeding a monocular geometric prior into the network: a separate geometry encoder (GGF) and a candidate-depth geometry correction (GeoCorr Lite). After freezing a reproducible baseline (A0f, 0.491 ± 0.009 mm overall, 3 seeds, full-resolution DTU), we tested the prior as an input, as a confidence-gated replacement and as a loss. As an input it did nothing measurable. The prior is too inaccurate in metric terms (median error 28 mm at the ambiguous pixels) to pick the right depth. Two later results changed the picture:
  1. A larger cost-volume network (B2) reaches 0.414 / 0.418 (2 seeds).
  2. On that network, the prior works as a training-only signal. Removing it worsens overall from 0.418 to 0.436 (seed 2, measured 2026-10-10), and most of the loss is on the hard scans.
We propose to change the paper's thesis to match: monocular foundation models should guide self-supervised MVS during training, targeted at the pixels where photometry is ambiguous, not enter the network as a depth input. This document records everything done so far, explains the baseline, places the idea against the closest published work, and sets out the experiments, success criteria and expected outcome for which approval is requested.
Novelty caveat, stated up front. Injecting frozen Depth-Anything-V2 features into the reference-view features (our running B2_mono_feat) is already published in supervised MVS (MonoMVSNet, ICCV 2025). Using a depth-foundation prior for label-free MVS is also published (DFM-MVS, arXiv 2025). So neither can be the paper's main claim. The proposed contribution is the ambiguity-targeted, scale-free use of the monocular prior inside the photometric self-supervised objective (Section 8.3), plus the diagnostic evidence for why the prior has to be used that way. Section 9 compares this with prior work in detail.

1. Problem and goal

Task. Multi-view stereo (MVS): given a reference image, several source images and known cameras, estimate a depth map per view, then fuse the depth maps into a 3D point cloud. We evaluate on the DTU benchmark: 22 test scans, scored by accuracy (prediction to ground truth), completeness (ground truth to prediction) and overall (their mean), in mm, lower is better.

Setting. Self-supervised (unsupervised) MVS. No ground-truth depth is used in training. The network learns from a photometric loss: if the predicted depth is right, warping source images into the reference view should reproduce the reference image. External pretrained models (here Depth-Anything-V2) are allowed as auxiliary priors and are disclosed as such.

Core difficulty. On textureless, reflective or repetitive surfaces, many depth candidates explain the images equally well, so the photometric signal cannot pick the true surface. We measured this directly (Section 5): at the failing pixels the training loss itself prefers a wrong depth. Any method that only reshapes the photometric objective cannot fix those pixels. Information has to come from outside the images' photometric consistency.

2. The original plan: geometry as an input (GeoFusionNet + GeoCorr Lite)

The design report (docs/architecture_comparison.md, docs/baseline_recovery_plan.md) proposed two ways to bring geometry into the network:

  • GeoFusionNet (representation level): a separate geometry encoder reads a geometric prior G (depth, normals, validity) and a Geometry-Guided Fusion (GGF) block adds its features to the RGB features as a zero-initialized residual.
  • GeoCorr Lite (scoring level): a small 1×1-conv head turns 6 to 8 scalar compatibility cues per (pixel, view, depth candidate) into a residual on the matching score, controlled by a learned reliability gate. Example cues: distance to the prior, reprojection error, normal disagreement.

Both were staged behind a reproduced baseline (Stage 0 → GGF → fixed GeoCorr → learned gate → view weighting → optional sampling), with zero-initialization so that each stage starts from the previous model.

The prior G is Depth-Anything-V2-Small relative inverse depth, anchored per view to metric mm by a RANSAC fit (1/z = s·g + t) to SIFT points triangulated with the known DTU cameras. No ground truth is used, so the self-supervised claim holds.

3. The baseline (Stage 0), explained

A reproducible baseline had to come first: an architectural gain means nothing if it is within the baseline's run-to-run spread. The frozen Stage 0 baseline is A0f (configs/ablations/A0f_fixes_only_short.yaml, frozen 2026-09-27).

3.1 Architecture

ComponentA0f (Stage 0)
Input5 views (1 reference + 4 source); training at 640×512, testing at 1600×1152
Feature encoderSmall RGB CNN, features at 1/4, 1/2 and full resolution
Cascade3 stages, CasMVSNet-style: 48 → 32 → 8 depth candidates at 1/4, 1/2 and 1 resolution. Each stage searches a narrowed window around the previous stage's depth.
Cost volumeVariance across views, reduced to 1 channel, into a 2-level 3D CNN regularizer. Two fixes we found and made default: out-of-view masking of source samples, and trilinear upsampling in the regularizer. Together they cut "sawtooth" probability artifacts from 41% to 0.1% of wrong pixels.
DepthSoftmax over candidates (higher score = better), expectation gives depth, and the probability gives confidence
Size121k parameters used (215k including the geometry encoder, which A0f does not use), peak test memory 12.7 GB at 1600×1152

3.2 Self-supervised objective

L = L_photo + 0.1·L_smooth (+ 0.5·L_geo in A0f). Each term:

  • L_photo: 0.85·SSIM + 0.15·L1 between the reference image and each warped source image, averaged over the 4 source views.
  • L_smooth: edge-aware smoothness on depth normalized by the per-image mean. Raw-mm smoothness was the cause of an early constant-depth failure, about 67 mm error.
  • L_geo: mean-normalized |D − G| on valid prior pixels.

3.3 Training chain (per seed)

  1. A0_baseline: 20k steps, constant lr 1e-3.
  2. A0_finetune_clmvs: 6k steps with CL-MVSNet's LR recipe (lr 5e-4, step decay).
  3. A0f: 1.5k steps with the cost-volume fixes on.

The whole chain is about 23.5 GPU-hours per seed on one NVIDIA A30.

3.4 Evaluation protocol

Fusion uses GeoMVSNet's open3d pipeline: probability > 0.3, 0.2 mm, at least 4 consistent views. Scoring uses the official DTUeval-python metric, cross-checked against the unmodified reference script. All 22 test scans at full resolution 1600×1152, 3 seeds.

All numbers measured before 2026-09-23 used a non-official metric with no fusion filtering, and are invalid.

5. Why geometry as an input failed

The diagnostic chain behind the decision to move away from the original design:

  1. The failure is an ambiguity, not a bug. On the outlier scans (11, 13, 48, 62, 77) the cost volume often has two or more competing minima. The photometric loss itself scores the wrong one as better. A more robust loss (L0.5, min-view), best-k view aggregation, wider search windows, an LR recipe and consistency distillation all failed to change this (Section 4).
  2. The prior's metric depth is too coarse to break the tie. At ambiguous pixels the competing dips are a median 60 mm apart, but G's error there is 27.7 mm. So G lands nearer the right dip only 52.4% of the time, about a coin flip. G comes within 4 mm (useful as a depth value) on just 11.5% of those pixels. Much of this error is per-view anchoring error in scale and shift from sparse SIFT points, not error in the monocular model's shape.
  3. The network ignores the input. Given as an input through the separate encoder (B1), G changed neither the depth error nor the competing-dip share beyond the control's own run-to-run swing. A zero-initialized branch that carries a coin-flip signal stays near zero.
  4. Hard gating helps only gross outliers. The one gate that passes fires mainly where the model is already wrong by more than 10 cm. It raises the share of pixels within 4 mm by −0.46 points, so it barely changes accuracy. On B2 it is nearly a no-op (0.418 → 0.415).
Conclusion. The prior's metric value is unreliable exactly where it would be needed, so a design that feeds it in as depth (GGF input, GeoCorr Lite's prior-distance cue, gating) has little to gain. What the monocular model does get right is relative structure: local ordering, smoothness and discontinuities. The new direction builds on that.

6. What did work

6.1 Capacity (B2)

B2 changes only the matching network: an FPN encoder (32/96/192), 8-group correlation cost, and a 2-level 3D U-Net regularizer, 2.03M parameters in total. Training chain, loss and protocol are unchanged.

Fused point clouds improve a lot (completeness 0.36 vs 0.52). Per-view depth metrics are slightly worse, though: val fine median 1.76 vs 1.52 mm. That mismatch is not yet explained (risk R4).

6.2 The prior as a training signal (new, measured 2026-10-10)

B2_nogeo is the full B2 chain (20k + 6k + 1.5k steps) with geo_weight 0, everything else identical, seed 2. Unlike the A0f no-geo test (a 1.5k-step continuation), the network never sees L_geo at any point.

8. The new direction

Thesis. In self-supervised MVS, a monocular depth foundation model is most useful as a training-time referee between the photometrically tied depth hypotheses found by the network's own cost volume, applied through scale-free relative-depth constraints. It should not be a depth input or a global pseudo-label. The trained MVS network needs no monocular model at test time.

8.2 Pipeline

8.3 C4: ambiguity-targeted, scale-free monocular guidance

The goal is to apply the monocular signal only where photometry cannot decide, and only in a form the monocular model gets right.

  1. Find the ambiguous pixels from the model itself. At each training step, mark a pixel ambiguous when the coarse-stage probability volume has a second local maximum within a ratio τ of the first, separated by at least k bins. This is the "competing dips" measure we already compute in scripts/costvol_diag.py, here computed without gradient and used as a mask. τ and k are fixed on dtu_val before any test evaluation.
  2. Use the monocular depth only up to a local affine fit, not as a metric value. In a window around each ambiguous pixel, fit scale and shift of DA-V2's inverse depth to the network's own confident depths. These are unambiguous pixels with high probability, so no SIFT anchoring and no ground truth are needed. The fitted local depth then serves as a referee among the K candidate dips.
  3. Train with a loss on the candidates, not a regression to G. Two variants, to be compared:
    • (a) Dip selection: cross-entropy on the probability volume that raises the dip nearest the locally fitted monocular depth, weighted by how clearly it wins.
    • (b) Relative ranking: a pairwise ordinal loss on sampled pixel pairs inside the ambiguous region, enforcing DA-V2's depth ordering.
  4. Leave confident pixels alone. Outside the mask, the objective is exactly the photometric baseline. This targets L5, the slight harm of global L_geo on easy scans.

Why this should work better than C2. Global L_geo pulls every pixel toward a metric G with a 5 to 69 mm median error. C4 asks the prior a different question: which of these two or three candidates is right? It answers in a local, scale-free frame calibrated by the network's own correct pixels. Lesson L2 says the metric answer is a coin flip. Whether the local, scale-free answer is better is measured cheaply before any training (gate G1).

What makes this a self-supervised contribution. No labels, and no SfM anchoring at training time. The prior plays the role of the missing tie-breaker, which is exactly the information the photometric loss lacks (L1).

Claimed contributions, if the plan succeeds:
  1. Diagnosis. A quantitative analysis showing that photometric self-supervision prefers wrong hypotheses on ambiguous surfaces, and that robust losses, view selection, consistency distillation and metric-prior inputs do not change this. This rests on measurements we already have (cost-profile probes, prior-vs-dip analysis, matched pairs).
  2. Method (C4). Ambiguity-targeted, scale-free monocular guidance inside photometric self-supervision, with no test-time monocular model and no metric anchoring.
  3. Finding. The value of a monocular training signal depends on matching capacity: no effect on the 121k-parameter model, a clear effect on the 2M-parameter one. This needs its own matched test (E7).
Not claimed as novel: frozen foundation-feature injection (C3), capacity changes (C1), global prior loss (C2).

The literature check is a first pass (2026-10-10, two web searches plus the MonoMVSNet and DFM-MVS paper pages). A systematic review of 2024 to 2026 self-supervised MVS papers that use monocular priors is item E0 and must be done before writing.
